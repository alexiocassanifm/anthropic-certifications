# 5.6 — Preserve information provenance and handle uncertainty in multi-source synthesis

> Task statement title reproduced from *Exam Guide v1.0*. Everything below is this project's own explanation.

**Domain:** [5 — Context Management & Reliability](../domains/5-context-management-and-reliability.md) (15%)

## What it tests

Keeping claims attached to their sources through a pipeline that naturally destroys the attachment — and what to do when sources disagree.

## Knowledge

**Source attribution is lost at the summarisation step**, when findings are compressed without preserving claim-source mappings. Once lost, it cannot be recovered downstream.

**Structured claim-source mappings** must be preserved *and merged* by the synthesis agent when combining findings.

**Conflicting statistics from credible sources are annotated with attribution, not arbitrarily resolved.** Picking one silently destroys information the reader needs.

**Temporal data:** requiring publication or collection dates in structured outputs prevents temporal differences being misread as contradictions. A 2023 figure and a 2025 figure are not a conflict; they are a trend.

## Skills

- Require subagents to output structured claim-source mappings — source URLs, document names, relevant excerpts — that downstream agents preserve through synthesis.
- **Structure reports with explicit sections distinguishing well-established findings from contested ones**, preserving original source characterisations and methodological context.
- **Complete document analysis with conflicting values included and explicitly annotated**, letting the coordinator decide how to reconcile before passing to synthesis.
- Require publication or data collection dates in structured outputs for correct temporal interpretation.
- **Render different content types appropriately in synthesis outputs** — financial data as tables, news as prose, technical findings as structured lists — rather than converting everything to a uniform format.

## Anti-patterns

- **Prose summaries between agents.** Metadata dissolves and cannot be reconstructed.
- **Silently picking one of two conflicting values.** The reader cannot tell a contested figure from an established one.
- **Resolving a conflict at the analysis stage.** The analysis agent lacks the context to decide; annotate and let the coordinator choose.
- **No dates in structured output.** Temporal differences get flagged as contradictions.
- **Flattening everything into a uniform format.** Financial data as prose is worse than financial data as a table.

## Exam angle

The recurring item: *"Two credible sources report different statistics for the same metric. What should the synthesis output do?"* → preserve both with source attribution and annotate the conflict. Any option that picks one, averages them, or drops the disagreement is wrong.

The temporal item is a close cousin and easy to miss: values differing because they were collected in different years are not contradictory. The fix is upstream — require dates in the structured output.

The provenance-loss item asks *where* attribution disappears. The answer is the summarisation step, which means the fix is upstream too: structured claim-source mappings that survive compression.

Distractor families: `suppresses-signal` (choosing one value silently), `solves-different-problem` (fixing the synthesis prompt when the mapping was never emitted).
